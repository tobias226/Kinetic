# KINETIC — a 90–120 second interview demonstration

**Start at the Welcome page**, not on a technical screen. This is about judgement and initiative rather than showing off a Python dashboard.

**0:00–0:20 — A thoughtful premise.**

> “In looking at Copley Point’s independent sponsor model, what stood out was that origination isn’t just a list of targets. You’re backing asset-intensive platforms and usually choosing the one institutional partner best aligned to each strategy. I thought about how a relationship-led team could turn that judgement into a repeatable process without making it impersonal.”

Click **Enter the intelligence workspace**.

**0:20–0:45 — Evidence, not generic CRM.**

Open **Market Signals**, showing a Q3 2026 logistics data point or Ofgem’s heat-network oversight, then **Origination Radar** to show how signals become an investment-screen hypothesis. Mention that all the pipeline companies are fictitious, but criteria come from Copley Point's public investment approach.

**0:45–1:20 — Signature feature.**

Open **Partnership Bridge**. Choose **Demo | Northgate Logistics Portfolio**. The app ranks illustrative capital partners for **one** platform, distinguishes thematic/capital fit from practical relationship access and highlights genuine diligence questions. Move the access-weight slider once. Select a partner and explain why **meeting the right investor** matters more than bulk outreach.

> “This is not an algorithm that tells us whom to invest with. It gives an analyst a defensible shortlist, surfaces missing information, then asks a person to record what they learnt and what to do next.”

**1:20–1:45 — A different angle.**

Open **Carbon Intelligence**. Change the holding period from 20 to 30 years, and point out the different carbon trajectories and very simple cash cost comparison.

> “I also read Copley Point’s paper on embodied carbon in industrial refurbishment. I wanted to reflect that thinking in an editable screen: assess the refurbishment question as both a long-term asset-management decision and a climate decision, rather than seeing ESG as a separate reporting exercise.”

**1:45–2:00 — Practical safeguards.**

Open **Partner Briefing Room**. Toggle the internal versus external audience to show that private internal rationale and internal-only JV milestones are excluded from the external version.

> “This is deliberately a synthetic, standalone demonstration. With real permissions, source data and a secure backend, it could become a working internal process. But relationships should stay personal, and no partner information should be shared by default.”

## If the conversation is time-constrained

Use **Welcome → Partnership Bridge → Carbon Intelligence**. The animated landing page is there to create immediate impact, but move quickly into the business logic so the technology supports rather than overwhelms your narrative. You can demonstrate the main value within 60 seconds.

## Likely interviewer challenges and strong answers

**“Why not just use Salesforce or DealCloud?”**  The prototype does not aim to replace an institutional CRM; it adds an explainable, strategy-specific decision layer for asset-platform origination. In production I would integrate with the system already used rather than force another data silo.

**“Where does the data come from?”**  All example deal and partner records are synthetic; public facts come from cited Copley Point, CBRE, Savills, Ofgem and government sources. The actual project would require approved CRM inputs, internal validation and licensed market feeds.

**“How do we ensure a partner wants this deal?”**  We cannot infer real appetite from a web scrape. The engine deliberately calls a mandate match a *hypothesis*, and a human must confirm current preferences, ticket size, capacity, investment committee process, exclusions and conflicts.

**“What would you build next?”**  A verified evidence log and trusted single source of truth, then secure permissions and a one-page decision history: *why we pursued a relationship, what we learned and what changed our thesis*.

**“Can co-partners log in?”**  Not in this public version. A genuine portal would require identity, data-room segregation, approvals, audit logs and permissions. The Partner Briefing Room demonstrates selective sharing, not a secure data room.

**“Did you build it yourself?”**  Be accurate about the technical assistance/tools you used. Emphasise your independent choice of business problem, source selection, hypotheses, testing and communication rather than overclaiming solo authorship.

## Delivery note

Consider sharing a live demo URL **only if asked** or at the end of the interview after explaining that it contains fictitious data. Do not send unsolicited public links containing actual firm branding, people or mandates. A small app illustrates initiative; it must not dominate discussion of judgement, empathy, commercial understanding and ability to build trusted relationships.
