# KINETIC — an accessible 90–120 second interview walkthrough

**Aim:** Show that you understand the *commercial workflow* behind Copley Point's public real-assets platform model. Do not focus on writing code. The landing-page turbine is a visual metaphor for moving from information to action; it is not evidence that KINETIC tracks real energy assets.

**0:00–0:15 — Welcome.** Start on the minimal animated introduction. Say:

> “I built an independent concept called KINETIC around a simple question: how can a relationship-led investment team turn the right information and connections into better conversations and investment opportunities?”

Click the **single Explore KINETIC** button. The next page has **three areas**.

**0:15–0:40 — Find opportunities.** Click **Explore opportunities**. Show the overview: where the fictional leads are in the process, the sectors represented and a clearly defined next action. Explain that the scores are illustrative checklists, not real underwriting judgements. If helpful, open **Market Signals** to show a dated, sourced research observation.

**0:40–1:20 — Build partnerships.** Click **← Three areas**, then **Explore partnerships**. This is the signature feature. Choose one fictional opportunity and examine the potential partner list. Point out that business fit matters more than how easily someone can get an introduction, and open the optional slider to demonstrate the trade-off *only if asked*.

> “The score is not the answer; it is the start of a conversation. It can compare a sector, capital requirement or potential introduction, but it cannot know whether an investor actually has capital available, trusts the management team or faces conflicts. Those are explicit checks a person needs to make.”

**1:20–1:45 — Assess value.** Return to **← Three areas** and click **Explore value**. Change the years or the upfront cost in the refurbishment-versus-rebuilding example.

> “I wanted a second question beyond finding investors: what are the longer-term consequences of investing in an asset? A refurbishment and a rebuild have different upfront costs and emissions, and the comparison should account for what happens later, too.”

**1:45–2:00 — Finish with judgement.**

> “This is intentionally not a live deal platform. The companies, investor profiles, relationships and figures are fictional, and the research section separates public evidence from assumptions. I see it as an example of how I would approach the role: research carefully, make connections thoughtfully and build practical tools without losing the human element.”

## When time is very short

Use **Welcome → Explore KINETIC → Build partnerships → Match opportunities to investors → six essential checks**. That makes your commercial thinking clear quickly.

## Questions you might receive

**“What does business fit mean?”** How well a fictional investor's stated interests and approximate capital size align with a fictional opportunity. It cannot tell us whether the investor genuinely wants the deal.

**“What does introduction weighting mean?”** The optional percentage controlling how much the demonstration rewards a possible existing relationship versus the investment-sector and capital fit. The default is 20% introduction, 80% business fit. The numbers are illustrative choices.

**“What else determines a match?”** Actual appetite and capital availability; return and risk expectations; management expertise; decision-making and governance; ESG or asset-level requirements; conflicts and exclusivity. These must be verified rather than guessed.

**“Is the climate tool a full financial model?”** No. It is a simplified emissions and cash-cost comparison with editable assumptions. It deliberately excludes valuation, rent, financing, discounting, tenant downtime, taxes and several lifecycle effects.

**“Does anyone at Copley Point use this?”** No. It is an independent, fictional interview prototype. Do not imply firm involvement, confidential access or production readiness.

**“Did you develop this yourself?”** Give an accurate account of any code-generation or design assistance you used. Emphasise your choice of business problem, research, review and demonstration, not solo authorship if it is inaccurate.
